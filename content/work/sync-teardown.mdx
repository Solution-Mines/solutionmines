## The failure mode

A sandbox is a simulation, and simulations are maintained with less care than
the thing they simulate. The differences that bite are rarely dramatic:

- **Pagination.** The sandbox has forty records, so nobody discovers the cursor
  is not stable under concurrent writes.
- **Nullability.** Fields that are always populated in test data are optional in
  production, and your parser has never seen a null.
- **Rate limits.** Often absent or far more generous in the sandbox, so the
  backoff path has never executed.
- **Webhook ordering.** Delivered neatly in sequence in test; out of order and
  occasionally twice in production.

## What we do instead

Sandbox tests verify the shape of the contract. Before launch we run one real,
reversible, low-value transaction against production and inspect the actual
payload against our parser. Where providers allow payload capture, we keep
those recordings and replay them in CI, so production reality becomes a
regression test rather than a one-off check.
