## How we approach it

Most software projects do not fail because a framework was wrong. They fail
because nobody wrote down what the system was supposed to do, the estimate was
a guess defended as a commitment, and the first integration with reality
happened in week ten.

So we front-load the parts that are cheap to change. Discovery produces a
written brief: the problem, the constraints, the data that already exists, and
the parts we are deliberately not building. It is the document we would want if
someone handed us this project cold.

### The hard parts we take a position on

**Estimates are ranges until discovery is done.** A fixed quote before anyone
has looked at the existing data is a sales tactic, not an estimate. We quote
fixed after discovery, and discovery itself is a small, separately-priced piece
of work you keep whether or not you continue with us.

**We build for the second developer.** The question is never "can we make this
work" — it is "can someone who was not here change this safely in a year". That
shapes how much indirection we add, how much we test, and how much we write
down.

**Working software beats status reports.** A staging URL from week one is worth
more than any amount of percentage-complete reporting, because it is the only
progress signal that cannot be fudged.
